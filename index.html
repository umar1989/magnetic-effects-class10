13.1Magnetic field and field lines
  • A compass needle is a small bar magnet. Its end pointing towards north is the north (north-seeking) pole; the other end is the south pole.
  • Like poles repel. Unlike poles attract.
  • Magnetic field: the region around a magnet in which its force can be detected. It has both magnitude and direction.
  • Direction of the field at a point: the direction in which the north pole of a compass needle points (moves) when placed there.
  • Field line: the path along which a free north pole would tend to move.
Properties of field lines
  1. They emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole outside the magnet. Inside the magnet they run from south to north, so they are closed curves.
  2. Crowded lines mean a stronger field (strongest near the poles). The compass deflection is larger near the poles.
  3. No two field lines cross. At a crossing point the compass would have to point two ways at once, which is impossible.
  4. The tangent to a line at any point gives the direction of the field there.
Exam tipAlways draw arrowheads. Outside the magnet: N to S. Inside: S to N. Never let two lines touch.

Activities: iron filings around a bar magnet (13.2) show the pattern; a small compass moved step by step traces one line (13.3).

IntroOersted's experiment
  • A compass placed near a straight wire is deflected when current flows (Activity 13.1). So a current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field.
  • Reversing the current reverses the deflection (Activity 13.4). So the field direction reverses too.
  • Textbook case: wire over the needle, current north to south, the needle's north pole moves towards the east. Reverse the current and it moves towards the west.
Exam tipConclusion to write: electricity and magnetism are related.
13.2.1–2Field due to a straight conductor
  • Iron filings form concentric circles around the wire (Activity 13.5). The circles are the field lines.
  • Larger current gives a larger field (the compass deflection increases).
  • Greater distance gives a weaker field (deflection decreases). The circles get larger and larger away from the wire.
  • Reversing the current reverses the direction of the field lines.
Right-hand thumb rule: imagine holding the wire in your right hand with the thumb pointing along the current. The fingers wrap around the wire in the direction of the field lines. (Also called Maxwell's corkscrew rule.)
Example 13.1Current east to west in a horizontal power line: the field turns clockwise when viewed from the east end and anticlockwise when viewed from the west end. Just above the wire it points north, just below it points south.
13.2.3Field due to a circular loop
  • Each point of the loop produces concentric circles that grow larger away from the wire. Near the centre they look like straight lines.
  • Every section of the loop adds to the field in the same direction inside the loop.
  • A coil of n turns gives n times the field of one turn, because the current in every turn has the same direction and the fields add up.
  • The field also depends directly on the current.
Exam tipClockwise current seen from above (loop on a table): field points into the table (downward) inside the loop and out of the table (upward) outside it.
13.2.4Solenoid and electromagnet
  • Solenoid: a coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder.
  • Its field pattern matches a bar magnet. One end behaves as a north pole, the other as a south pole.
  • Inside, the field lines are parallel straight lines, so the field is uniform (same at all points).
  • Electromagnet: a core of soft iron wrapped with a coil of insulated copper wire. The solenoid's field magnetises the soft iron.
  • Finding the poles with a bar magnet: bring the north pole of the bar magnet near one end. If the end repels it, that end is a north pole. If it attracts, that end is a south pole.
Exam tipQuick pole check: look at one end. Anticlockwise current means that end is north. Clockwise means south.
13.3Force on a current-carrying conductor
  • A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force (Activity 13.7: an aluminium rod AB between the poles of a horse-shoe magnet is displaced).
  • Reversing the current, or reversing the field, reverses the direction of the force.
  • The force is largest when the current is at right angles to the field. It is then perpendicular to both.
  • The force increases with a larger current, a stronger magnet and a longer rod (Q2, in-text).
Fleming's left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular. Forefinger = direction of magnetic field. Middle finger = direction of current. Thumb = direction of force (motion) on the conductor.
  • Current direction is taken opposite to the motion of electrons. Use this for electron-beam questions.
  • A charged particle moving in a magnetic field feels a force perpendicular to its velocity. Its direction changes, but its speed does not. Velocity and momentum change.
  • Devices: electric motor, generator, loudspeaker, microphone, measuring instruments.
Common mistakeUsing the right hand for a motor. Force on a conductor is always the left hand.
13.4Electric motor
Electric motor: a rotating device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. Principle: a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.
  • Parts: rectangular coil ABCD of insulated copper wire; two poles of a magnet (arms AB and CD perpendicular to the field); split ring halves P and Q joined to the coil ends; stationary brushes X and Y touching P and Q; axle; battery.
  • Working: current enters through brush X and leaves through Y. In arm AB it flows A to B; in CD it flows C to D, the opposite way. By Fleming's left-hand rule AB is pushed down and CD is pushed up, so the coil turns anticlockwise.
  • After half a turn Q touches X and P touches Y. The current reverses (path DCBA), so the forces reverse too, and the coil keeps turning the same way.
  • Split ring = commutator: a device that reverses the direction of current through the circuit. It reverses the current every half rotation.
  • Commercial motors use (i) an electromagnet instead of a permanent magnet, (ii) a large number of turns, (iii) a soft iron core. Core plus coil is the armature.
  • Uses: fans, refrigerators, mixers, washing machines, computers, MP3 players.
13.5Electromagnetic induction
Electromagnetic induction: the process by which a changing magnetic field in a conductor induces a current in another conductor (production of induced current in a coil when the magnetic field linked with it changes).
  • Activity 13.8: pushing a magnet into a coil gives a momentary galvanometer deflection one way; pulling it out deflects the other way; holding it still gives zero. Moving the coil instead of the magnet gives the same effects.
  • Using the south pole reverses all the deflections.
  • Activity 13.9: coil-1 (primary) with battery and key, coil-2 (secondary) with galvanometer. A momentary current appears in coil-2 only when the current in coil-1 starts or stops (changes). Steady current gives no deflection.
  • Ways to induce current: move the coil in a magnetic field, move a magnet relative to the coil, or change the magnetic field around the coil.
  • The induced current is highest when the motion is at right angles to the field.
  • A galvanometer detects the presence of a current. Its pointer rests at zero (centre) and deflects left or right with the direction of current.
Fleming's right-hand rule: thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the right hand mutually perpendicular. Forefinger = magnetic field. Thumb = motion of the conductor. Middle finger = induced current.
13.6Electric generator, AC and DC
Generator: converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. Principle: electromagnetic induction.
  • Parts: rotating rectangular coil ABCD between the poles of a permanent magnet; two rings R1 and R2 on an axle; stationary brushes B1 and B2 pressed on the rings; external circuit with a galvanometer.
  • Rotating the coil makes arms AB and CD cut the field. Fleming's right-hand rule gives the induced current direction. More turns add up to a larger current.
  • After every half rotation the induced current in each arm reverses. Current that reverses at equal intervals is alternating current (AC). This is an AC generator (slip rings).
  • With a split-ring commutator one brush always touches the arm moving up and the other the arm moving down, so the output is in one direction: direct current (DC).
  • Function of brushes: they stay in contact with the rotating rings (or split ring) and carry the current to the external circuit.
  • India: AC of 220 V and 50 Hz, direction changes every 1/100 s. Most power stations produce AC.
  • Advantage of AC: power can be sent over long distances without much loss of energy.
  • Sources of DC: cell, battery, DC generator. Sources of AC: AC generators (alternators) at power stations, household mains.
Exam tipThe current changes direction once in each half revolution.
13.7Domestic electric circuits and safety
  • Mains supply passes into the house through the electricity meter, a main fuse and the main switch, then to separate circuits.
  • Live wire (positive): red insulation. Neutral wire (negative): black insulation. Potential difference between them: 220 V. Earth wire: green insulation, joined to a metal plate deep in the earth.
  • Two usual circuits: 15 A for high-power appliances (geysers, air coolers) and 5 A for bulbs and fans.
  • Appliances are connected in parallel across live and neutral so each gets the same potential difference, with its own switch.
  • Earth wire: gives a low-resistance path for leakage current. It keeps the metal body of an appliance (electric press, toaster, table fan, refrigerator) at the earth's potential, so the user does not get a severe shock.
  • Fuse: protects circuits and appliances from overloading and short-circuiting. Joule heating melts the fuse wire and breaks the circuit.
  • Short circuit: live and neutral wires touch directly (damaged insulation or a fault in the appliance). The current rises abruptly.
  • Overloading can be caused by short circuit, an accidental rise in supply voltage, or too many appliances on one socket.
RememberUse the NCERT colours in the board exam: live red, neutral black, earth green. Newer wiring codes in homes can differ, but the textbook answer is what is marked.
Magnetism in medicineWeak ion currents in nerves produce tiny magnetic fields (about one-billionth of the earth's). The heart and brain fields are the most significant. The idea behind MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) uses the magnetic field inside the body to image body parts.

Labs

Drag, flip and switch things. Each lab ends with the sentence you can write in the exam.

What to notice

  • Drag the compass. Its red end (north) always points along the field line, away from the north pole of the magnet.
  • Near either pole the field is strong and the lines are crowded. Far away it is weak.
  • Inside the magnet the lines run south to north, so every line is a closed curve.
  • Lines never cross.

Write this

Field lines emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole; inside the magnet they go from south to north.

What to notice

  • The wire is seen end-on. ⊗ means current into the page, ⊙ means out of the page.
  • The field lines are concentric circles. Drag the compass outward: the circles get larger and the field weaker.
  • Raise the current: the field is stronger. Reverse it: all arrows reverse.
  • Right-hand thumb rule: thumb along the current, fingers curl along the field.

Write this

The field around a straight wire is a set of concentric circles; it increases with current and decreases with distance.

What to notice

  • Loop: inside the loop every section of wire pushes the field the same way, and at the centre the lines are straight. n turns give n times the field.
  • Solenoid: inside, the lines are parallel and evenly spaced (uniform field). Outside, the pattern is that of a bar magnet.
  • Reverse the current and the poles swap.
  • Add the soft iron core: the solenoid becomes an electromagnet.
Field BCurrentForce or motion

How to read it

  • Right, up and towards you are drawn as in a normal 3-D sketch. For compass questions read Right = East, Left = West, Into the page = North, Towards you = South.
  • Left hand: thumb = force, forefinger = field, middle finger = current.
  • Right hand: thumb = motion, forefinger = field, middle finger = induced current.
  • If current and field are parallel the force is zero. It is greatest at 90°.
  • For an electron, the current points opposite to its velocity.

Practice: left-hand rule

What to notice

  • End view: the two arms AB and CD are wires going into and out of the page. The magnet's field runs horizontally.
  • The arms carry opposite currents, so the forces are opposite: one arm is pushed up, the other down. That turns the coil.
  • Every time the coil passes the vertical position the split ring swaps the brush contacts, the currents reverse, and the coil keeps turning the same way.
  • Switch the split ring off (plain rings): the coil swings to the vertical position and stops.
  • Swap the magnet's poles: the coil turns the other way.

Write this

The split ring (commutator) reverses the current in the coil every half rotation so the torque always acts in the same direction.

What to notice

  • The coil is turned by an outside force (mechanical energy in, electrical energy out).
  • Output is largest when the arms move straight across the field (coil plane parallel to the field) and zero when the coil is perpendicular to the field.
  • Slip rings: the output reverses every half turn. That is AC.
  • Split ring: the output keeps one direction. That is DC. The current inside the coil still reverses; the commutator swaps the connections.
  • Turn faster: larger output and more cycles per second.

What to notice

  • Drag the magnet towards the coil: the galvanometer needle jumps to one side (say right).
  • Drag it away: the needle jumps to the other side.
  • Stop: the needle returns to zero. A magnet held still inside the coil gives no current.
  • Move the coil instead of the magnet: same result. Only relative motion matters.
  • Present the south pole instead: every deflection reverses.
  • Faster motion gives a bigger deflection.

Write this

Relative motion between a magnet and a coil induces a current in the coil (electromagnetic induction).

What to notice

  • Coil-1 (primary, more turns) is joined to a battery and key. Coil-2 (secondary) is joined only to the galvanometer.
  • Switch on: the needle jumps one way and returns to zero.
  • Switch off: the needle jumps the opposite way and returns.
  • Steady current, on or off: no deflection. Slide the current up or down while it is on: deflection appears only while it changes.

Write this

A changing current in the primary changes the magnetic field linked with the secondary, so a current is induced in the secondary.

Circuit rating

What to notice

  • Current drawn = power ÷ voltage (I = P ÷ V) at 220 V.
  • A 5 A circuit can carry 220 × 5 = 1100 W. A 15 A circuit can carry 220 × 15 = 3300 W.
  • Add a 2 kW oven to the 5 A circuit: current is about 9.1 A, more than 5 A. The circuit is overloaded and the fuse melts (Joule heating).
  • Appliances are in parallel, so each gets 220 V and has its own switch.
  • The earth wire connects the metal body to the ground through a low-resistance path.

Wattages are typical values chosen for practice.

Short circuit vs overloading

  • Short circuit: live and neutral touch directly. Current rises abruptly.
  • Overloading: too much current for the circuit. Causes: short circuit, a sudden rise in supply voltage, too many appliances on one socket.
  • Fuse: Joule heating melts the wire and breaks the circuit.

Safety measures

  • Fuse in every domestic circuit.
  • Earth wire for metallic appliances.
  • Do not connect too many appliances to one socket.
  • Use the 15 A circuit for high-power appliances.

Questions and answers

All in-text and exercise questions of the chapter with model answers. Bold words are the scoring keywords.

Long answers (3 to 5 marks)
Draw a labelled diagram of an electric motor. Explain its principle and working. What is the function of a split ring?5

Diagram labels: rectangular coil ABCD, N and S poles, split ring halves P and Q (commutator), brushes X and Y, axle, battery, direction of current in AB and CD.

Principle: a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.

  1. Current from the battery enters through brush X, flows A to B in arm AB and C to D in arm CD (opposite directions), and leaves through brush Y.
  2. By Fleming's left-hand rule AB is pushed down and CD is pushed up. The coil rotates anticlockwise.
  3. After half a rotation the halves Q and P change brushes. The current reverses (DCBA) and so do the forces. The coil continues in the same direction.

Function of split ring: it acts as a commutator; it reverses the direction of current in the coil after every half rotation so the coil keeps rotating in the same direction.

Explain the principle and working of an electric generator with a labelled diagram. What is the function of brushes?5

Diagram labels: rectangular coil ABCD, N and S poles, rings R1 and R2, brushes B1 and B2, axle, galvanometer (external circuit).

Principle: electromagnetic induction. Mechanical energy rotates a conductor in a magnetic field and produces electricity.

  1. The axle is rotated from outside so arm AB moves up and CD moves down in the field.
  2. By Fleming's right-hand rule induced currents flow along AB and CD, so the current flows in direction ABCD and in the external circuit from B2 to B1.
  3. After half a rotation the arms swap roles, the current becomes DCBA and flows from B1 to B2 outside. The current reverses every half rotation: alternating current.
  4. Replacing the rings by a split-ring commutator gives direct current.

Function of brushes: they stay pressed on the rotating rings and conduct the current from the coil to the external circuit.

Distinguish between AC and DC. State one advantage of AC.3

DC always flows in one direction. AC reverses its direction periodically (in India every 1/100 s, frequency 50 Hz).

Advantage of AC: electric power can be transmitted over long distances without much loss of energy.

What is electromagnetic induction? Describe how current can be induced in a coil.3

The production of an induced current in a coil placed in a region where the magnetic field changes with time.

  • Move a magnet towards or away from the coil (or move the coil near the magnet): the galvanometer deflects while there is relative motion.
  • Change the current in a nearby coil (switch on, off, or vary it): the changing field induces a current in the secondary coil.

In-text questions
Why does a compass needle get deflected when brought near a bar magnet? (13.1)2

A compass needle is itself a small bar magnet. The bar magnet has a magnetic field around it and this field exerts a force on the poles of the needle. Unlike poles attract and like poles repel, so the needle turns and is deflected.

Draw magnetic field lines around a bar magnet. (13.2.2)2

Draw closed curves leaving the north pole and entering the south pole outside the magnet, with arrowheads. Inside the magnet draw lines from south to north. Crowd the lines near the poles. Keep every line separate.

List the properties of magnetic field lines.3
  1. They start at the north pole and end at the south pole outside the magnet; inside they go from south to north (closed curves).
  2. The direction of the field at a point is the direction in which a north pole would move; the tangent gives the direction.
  3. Crowded lines show a stronger field.
  4. No two field lines cross.
Why don't two magnetic field lines intersect each other?2

If two lines crossed, a compass needle placed at the point of intersection would have to point in two directions at the same time. That is impossible, because the field has only one direction at a point.

A circular loop lies in the plane of the table. The current is clockwise. Apply the right-hand rule to find the direction of the field inside and outside the loop. (13.2.4)2

Seen from above, curling the fingers of the right hand clockwise puts the thumb pointing downward. So the field is into the plane of the table (downward) inside the loop and out of the plane (upward) outside the loop.

The magnetic field in a given region is uniform. Draw a diagram to represent it.2

Draw parallel, equally spaced, straight lines with the same direction everywhere (for example horizontal lines with arrowheads to the right). The field inside a long solenoid looks like this.

The magnetic field inside a long straight solenoid carrying current: (a) is zero (b) decreases towards its end (c) increases towards its end (d) is the same at all points.1

(d) is the same at all points. The field lines inside are parallel straight lines, so the field is uniform.

Which property of a proton can change while it moves freely in a magnetic field? (a) mass (b) speed (c) velocity (d) momentum1

(c) velocity and (d) momentum. The force is perpendicular to the motion, so it changes the direction of motion (velocity and momentum are vectors) but not the speed. Mass does not change.

In Activity 13.7, how will the displacement of rod AB be affected if (i) the current is increased, (ii) a stronger magnet is used, (iii) the rod is longer?3

In all three cases the force on the rod increases, so the displacement increases. Force depends on the current, the field strength and the length of the conductor in the field.

An alpha particle projected towards west is deflected towards north by a magnetic field. The field is: (a) towards south (b) towards east (c) downward (d) upward1

(d) upward. An alpha particle is positive, so the current is along its motion (west). Fleming's left-hand rule: middle finger west, thumb north, so the forefinger (field) points up.

State Fleming's left-hand rule. (13.4)2

Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand so they are mutually perpendicular. If the forefinger points in the direction of the magnetic field and the middle finger in the direction of the current, then the thumb points in the direction of motion (force) of the conductor.

What is the principle of an electric motor?1

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force, which rotates the coil.

What is the role of the split ring in an electric motor?2

It is a commutator. It reverses the direction of current in the coil every half rotation, so the force on each arm reverses and the coil continues to rotate in the same direction.

Explain different ways to induce current in a coil. (13.5)2
  • Move a magnet towards or away from the coil.
  • Move the coil in a magnetic field (relative motion).
  • Change the current in a nearby coil so the magnetic field around the coil changes.
State the principle of an electric generator. (13.6)1

Electromagnetic induction: when a coil rotates in a magnetic field, a current is induced in it.

Name some sources of direct current.1

Cell, battery, DC generator.

Which sources produce alternating current?1

AC generators (alternators) at power stations; household mains supply is AC.

A rectangular coil of copper wire is rotated in a magnetic field. The direction of the induced current changes once in each: (a) two revolutions (b) one revolution (c) half revolution (d) one-fourth revolution1

(c) half revolution.

Name two safety measures commonly used in electric circuits and appliances. (13.7)2

Earthing (earth wire for metallic appliances) and the electric fuse.

An electric oven of 2 kW is operated on a 220 V domestic circuit with a current rating of 5 A. What result do you expect? Explain.3

Current drawn: I = P ÷ V = 2000 ÷ 220 ≈ 9.1 A.

This is more than the 5 A rating, so the circuit is overloaded. The fuse will melt (Joule heating) and break the circuit. The oven needs the 15 A circuit.

What precaution should be taken to avoid overloading of domestic circuits?2
  • Do not connect too many appliances to a single socket.
  • Use a fuse of the correct rating.
  • Keep the insulation of wires in good condition.
  • Connect high-power appliances to the 15 A circuit.

Ex 18. What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it necessary to earth metallic appliances?2

The earth wire gives a low-resistance conducting path to the ground for any leakage current. A leak to the metal body keeps the body at the potential of the earth, so the user does not get a severe electric shock.
